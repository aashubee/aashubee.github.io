APE — A Portable Emulator

Project Overview

APE is a handheld, battery-powered embedded security and RF research tool built by the HOPE collective, inspired by the Flipper Zero but designed with higher-performance analog, RF, and power capabilities. The device supports multiple wireless and sensing modalities — Bluetooth Low Energy, sub-GHz radio, RFID read/emulation, and IR transmit/receive — all in a compact form factor powered by a single-cell Li-ion or LiFePO₄ battery.

The system uses USB-C Power Delivery to negotiate input power, enabling simultaneous operation and charging via an integrated BMS and charging IC, with a fuel gauge for accurate state-of-charge monitoring. An STM32-class microcontroller manages user interaction through an LCD/OLED display, directional pad, status LEDs, and optional haptics, while interfacing with external flash and a microSD card for firmware and data storage. The board has been designed and ordered — bring-up and testing is the next phase.

System Architecture

The board is partitioned into four major domains, each with distinct design requirements:

  • Power — USB-C PD negotiation, single-cell battery charging + protection, 3.3 V and 5 V boost regulators, fuel gauge. Designed for simultaneous charge + run with proper load sharing.
  • Digital / MCU — STM32-class MCU with SWD/JTAG debug port, external flash, microSD. Drives display, buttons, LEDs, and haptics.
  • RF / Analog — Sub-GHz radio, RFID antenna front-end, IR blaster + receiver, BLE at 2.4 GHz. Mixed-signal layout with careful domain separation.
  • User Interface — LCD or OLED display (high-resolution), directional pad, power button, status LEDs, optional haptic motor.

My Role — RFID Subsystem

My contribution was the RFID subsystem — designing the analog front-end for reading and emulating RFID tags using a custom loop antenna. This involved IC selection, antenna matching network design, and layout decisions to isolate the sensitive analog RF path from digital noise on the rest of the board. The RFID subsystem satisfies the analog design requirement for the project, as it involves continuous-time signal conditioning, impedance matching, and antenna resonance tuning.

Design Challenges

The biggest challenges in this design center on RF coexistence and mixed-signal integrity. Running BLE (2.4 GHz), sub-GHz, and RFID simultaneously on one board requires careful antenna placement, ground plane management, and shielding strategy to prevent inter-modulation and desensitization. Power management also presented non-trivial challenges — USB-PD negotiation in a compact battery-powered system requires coordinating the charger IC, fuel gauge, and boost regulators under varying load conditions without dropping out or creating ground bounce that affects sensitive analog sections.

Build Process

After finalizing the schematic and layout in KiCad, the board was ordered and partially assembled. Bring-up and functional testing of each subsystem — starting with power rails, then MCU, then RF — is the next step.

Team

APE was built by the HOPE collective — a group of Berkeley engineers who each owned a subsystem end-to-end, from schematic to layout to bring-up.
